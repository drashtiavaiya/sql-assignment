SESSION 3 : DOMAIN UNDERSTANDING FOR ANALYTICS

Task 1  Why domain knowledge matters

I chose Flipkart because e-commerce data can be misleading without an understanding of the order journey. A placed order is not necessarily a completed sale: it may be cancelled, returned or refunded. I would clarify how each stage is recorded before measuring sales, so the analysis reflects what the business actually earned.

Task 2 : Four KPIs for Flipkart

1. Net revenue. Sales from completed orders after cancellations and refunds. This gives a more reliable view of sales than the value of all orders placed.
2. Profit margin. The share of revenue left after costs. It shows whether higher sales are also improving profitability.
3. Average order value. Net revenue divided by completed orders. This helps track changes in customer spending per order.
4. Repeat customer rate. The percentage of customers who make another purchase. It helps assess whether Flipkart is retaining customers beyond their first order.

Task 3 : Customer acquisition cost for Paytm

Paytm could calculate customer acquisition cost for a campaign by dividing its acquisition spending by the number of new customers gained. If it spends ₹80,000 on advertising and ₹20,000 on sign-up incentives, and the campaign brings in 400 new customers, the CAC is (₹80,000 + ₹20,000) ÷ 400 = ₹250 per customer. I would count only customers acquired through that campaign, using the same period for both spending and sign-ups.

Task 4 : A new metric for Swiggy

I would introduce a Smooth Delivery Rate: the percentage of completed orders delivered by the promised time without a delivery-related complaint.

Smooth Delivery Rate = (On-time orders with no delivery complaint ÷ Completed orders) × 100

On-time delivery alone may miss problems such as poor handoffs or difficulty contacting a rider. Tracking this rate by area and time of day could help Swiggy identify where the delivery experience needs attention.
